Set up RCMP in 10 minutes

RCMP tracks your weight, food, training and recovery in one place and tells you each day what to change. This guide walks you through installing it, connecting your iPhone and Fitbod, and what every part of the app does.

Step 1 · 2 minutes

Install the app

RCMP isn’t in the App Store. It’s a web app you add to your Home Screen, and from then on it opens like any other app.

  1. Open c26b8w67hc-jpg.github.io/rcpm in Safari on your iPhone. Chrome works on Android.
  2. Tap the Share button (the square with the arrow), then Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  3. Close Safari and open RCMP from your Home Screen.
  4. Enter your invite code (6 letters and numbers, from Bence), then your first name, then read and accept the privacy note.

Already joined in Safari before adding it to the Home Screen? The Home Screen app starts empty. Just enter the same code again: it keeps working for 24 hours after you first use it and signs you into the same account.

Step 2 · 3 minutes

The first-time setup

Five short screens. Your answers set your calories, protein and training program, so be honest. You can change everything later in Setup.

Step 3 · 5 minutes Recommended

Connect Apple Health

This lets your iPhone (and Apple Watch, if you have one) send numbers to RCMP every hour, so you don’t type them in. It uses an app called Health Auto Export, which reads Apple Health and sends it to your private RCMP address.

What gets sent, and what RCMP does with it

From Apple HealthUsed for
StepsThe Steps ring and the daily steps goal on To do.
Weight, body fatFills in your weigh-in if you use a smart scale that writes to Health.
Sleep (watch)The Sleep ring and the recovery advice.
Resting heart rate, HRV (watch)Spots when you’re under-recovered, then the session suggestion holds your weights and drops a set.
Active + resting energy (watch)What you burned, compared with what you ate.
Workouts (watch)Runs, rides, Pilates and classes of 20+ minutes show up as sessions. Gym workouts give your Fitbod session its length and calories.

No Apple Watch? Steps and weight still work. Sleep and heart numbers need a watch (or a sleep app that writes to Health).

How to set it up

  1. In RCMP: Setup › Your connections › Apple Health › Set up, then tap Copy address. This is your private address; don’t share it.
  2. Install Health Auto Export – JSON+CSV from the App Store. The automatic sending (“Automations”) is part of its paid upgrade, so unlock that.
  3. Open it and allow access to Apple Health when it asks. Turn on everything in the list below.
  4. Go to Automations, tap + (new automation) and choose REST API.
  5. URL: paste the address you copied.
  6. Data Type: Health Metrics. Select: Step Count, Weight & Body Mass, Body Fat Percentage, Sleep Analysis, Resting Heart Rate, Heart Rate Variability, Active Energy, Resting Energy.
  7. Export Format: JSON. Export Version: Version 2. Aggregate data / interval: Days. Sync cadence: every hour.
  8. Turn the automation on and save it.
  9. Make a second automation exactly the same way, but with Data Type Workouts.
  10. Tap Run now (or Manual export) on each automation once so the first batch goes straight away.

Check it worked: in RCMP, tap Sync at the top. Setup › Your connections should now say “Last delivery …”, and the Steps ring on Today fills in.

iPhone only lets apps send in the background now and then. If RCMP says “Apple Health went quiet”, open Health Auto Export once and it catches up.

Step 4 · 2 minutes If you use Fitbod

Connect Fitbod

Fitbod has every set you’ve lifted. Importing it gives RCMP your lift history, and from that it calculates the weight and reps for your next session, flags new records and spots lifts that have stalled.

  1. In Fitbod, open the Log tab and tap the settings cog (top right).
  2. Scroll down to Export Workout Data and save the CSV file to Files (or email it to yourself).
  3. In RCMP: Setup › Your connections › Fitbod › Import CSV (or Progress › Training › Import Fitbod) and pick the file. Each day becomes one gym session with every working set.

Fitbod can’t send automatically, so export again once a week (Sunday is a good habit). Importing again only adds or updates; nothing gets duplicated. RCMP reminds you on Today when a gym session from Apple Health has no exercises yet.

Don’t use Fitbod? That’s fine: log sessions in RCMP itself (see Logging), or follow RCMP’s own program.

Logging food and training

Food

  • Add meal, then search a food (basics are built in in English and Spanish, and supermarket products come from Open Food Facts). Pick it and type the grams.
  • Or describe the meal in words (“chicken, rice and salad, a big plate”) and let the AI estimate it.
  • Eat it again re-adds a meal you’ve logged before in one tap. Most days are repeats, so this gets fast.
  • Suggest on the Fuel card picks one of your usual meals that fits what’s left today.

Training

  • Suggest on the Training card shows today’s session from your program: exercises, sets, reps and weights, each with a short form cue. Tap Log as today’s session, adjust it to what you actually did, and save after training.
  • Weights follow double progression: stay at a weight until every set hits the top of the rep range, then the next session adds the smallest jump.
  • No weight shown yet? Pick one you could do two more reps with. From your second session on, RCMP fills it in.
  • Add session logs anything else: a class, a run, a gym session by hand.

Progress

  • Weight: the 7-day average (daily weight jumps 1 kg on water alone, so the average is what counts), pace toward your goal, and body measurements with a body-fat estimate.
  • Training: your program, the week ahead, sessions per week and each lift’s progress.
  • Food: your averages, and after three weeks your real daily burn and a corrected calorie target.
  • Recovery: sleep, resting heart rate and HRV against your own normal.
  • Lab: small experiments (does late coffee hurt your sleep?) scored on your own data.

The coach

Ask anything in the Coach tab: “how much protein should I eat?”, “what should I train today?”, “alternative to bench press”, “my knee hurts on squats”. Common questions are answered instantly from your own numbers. The AI coach takes the rest, within a small monthly budget that resets on the 1st (Setup shows how much is left). The built-in answers always work.

For pain or injuries the coach will always point you to a physio or doctor.

Setup

  • Account: who you’re signed in as, and the AI budget left this month.
  • Your connections: Apple Health and Fitbod.
  • Daily targets, Goal: calories, macros, goal weight and date, gym days, steps goal, carb cycling on or off.
  • Profile and program: goal type, body, experience, equipment, joints to go easy on, city and supermarket (used for meal plans). Recalculate targets redoes your calories from your latest weight.
  • Supplement stack: what you take, so you can tick it off daily.
  • Your data: download a backup any time.

Your data and privacy

  • Your log is stored privately in a Google Drive folder that belongs to Bence, who runs RCMP. Only you and Bence can technically access it; it isn’t shared with anyone else.
  • Apple Health numbers you send are copied into that same folder.
  • When the AI coach answers, your question and the relevant numbers go to Anthropic’s Claude API. Built-in answers never leave the app.
  • Ask Bence any time to export or delete your data.
